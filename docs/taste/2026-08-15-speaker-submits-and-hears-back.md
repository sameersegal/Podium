# S2 — Get a talk in, and find out whether it got in

`speaker-submits-and-hears-back`

Walked 15 August 2026 against the shipped seed (DevFlow Conf 2027), three sittings, phone then
laptop. Journal and screenshots from this run are in `.walk/`; every screenshot named below is from
it. A narrower cut of this journey was walked on 14 August; I did not read that report until after
my ratings were written, and what it changed is in the last section, which changes nothing above it.

## Who I was

**Alex Mercer**, Staff Engineer at Northwind Freight, where I look after the build and release
tooling forty teams depend on. I have never spoken at a conference and have never seen this product.
A newsletter linked to a call for proposals and I clicked it on a Sunday evening, on my phone, on
the sofa, because I gave this talk internally in June and people kept asking for the slides.

**What I came to do:** get my talk in before it closes, and then find out.

**What done looks like to me:** a screen that says the talk is in — something I could screenshot and
send my manager — and enough of a sense of what happens next that I would not email anybody.

**What I would have done instead:** emailed the organizer directly, or submitted to the other
conference in October. The bar for *not bothering* is low. It is Sunday, this is unpaid, and nobody
is expecting me to do it.

**The trip:** Sunday evening, phone (390 × 844), interrupted part-way. Tuesday, laptop (1440 × 900),
**new device** — profile deleted, nothing but what the product stored on its own side. Then a third
sitting on the same laptop, to find out what was happening to it.

## Did I finish?

**I finished. Alex would not have.**

The talk is in. But between sitting 1 and sitting 2 the product put a wall between a first-time
speaker and their own half-written draft, and I hit that wall four times. The scenario's stop rule
is three. I only got past it by typing a URL I half-remembered from Sunday's address bar — and the
price of that guess was a **second, duplicate proposal** and re-typing every answer. A real Alex
closes the tab at wall three and the conference never learns the talk existed.

## What took more than one attempt

Seven re-dos, in the order they happened.

1. **Filling in the form on the public page.** It is right there, the fields accept typing in the
   page's own serif, and the only button is at the bottom of it. I typed five answers into it and
   pressed the button; every one was dropped (`.walk/02-cfp-preview-typed-into.png`). The caption
   *nothing here is saved until you start a submission* is honest and is eight phone-screens above
   the button, and it reads two ways — *don't bother* or *we'll save it when you start*.
2. **Starting.** The button says *Start a submission* and lands on **Sign in**, whose primary action
   is the one thing a first-timer cannot do. *Create one* is twelve-pixel grey text under it.
3. **Reading the sign-up blurb twice** to work out whether the account I was making could submit.
4. **Reading the ACCEPTED badge three times.**
5. **Reading `Draft. Nothing is waiting on you.` against `now ● Submitted` three times.**
6. **Undoing an answer I never gave.** Step 3's required *May we record and publish this session?*
   let me straight past unticked, and the review screen then reported it as a definite **No**. Four
   actions to go back and make it Yes. Without the review screen I would have shipped a refusal to
   be recorded that I never meant.
7. **The four walls** (below), which is the re-do the journey actually died on.

## What was not intuitive

Where what I did was reasonable and the product expected something else.

- **I looked for my draft in the portal.** It is on the screen, named, with its status. It is not a
  link. `Your talks` in the nav points at the page I was already on; `Profile` has nothing;
  *Start a submission* on the CFP page redirects to that same dead-end portal; `/portal/proposals`
  redirects to it too. Four walls (`.walk/10`, `.walk/11`, `.walk/12`).
- **I read `No questions on this step` and budgeted for a form that stopped at step 2.** Step 3 has
  three questions, one of them required (`.walk/15-step3-logistics.png`), and step 2 has a fifth
  field the preview never showed. The page's whole promise is that you can size up the job before
  you sign up, and it under-declared the job by four questions.
- **I expected `Start a proposal` to know which call I meant.** I got there by clicking a link on
  that call's own page and was then shown a screen asking me to choose between one option.
- **I expected the review screen's Submit button to say what it does.** Nothing under it says
  whether submitting is final. The sentence that answers it — and answers it beautifully — only
  appears *after* you have submitted.
- **On the conference's own front page, signed in, I could not find my talk.** The nav offers
  *Profile*, which goes to `/portal/profile`, and only from there is there a *Your talks*. Two
  guesses (`.walk/19-sitting3-event-home.png`).
- **That same front page's one call-to-action, `Submit a talk — closes 10 Apr`, goes to the sponsor
  call**, not the open public one that closes 30 April. Had I used the front door on Sunday instead
  of the newsletter link, I would have aimed my talk at the wrong call.

## Moments of care

Real ones, and they are the reason this product is a small number of changes from being much better
than it scored.

- **The whole form is inspectable before you sign up.** Four steps, every question, working
  dropdowns, on the public page (`.walk/01-cfp-public-phone.png`). I have not seen another CFP do
  this and it is the only reason I started.
- **"First-time speakers are welcome and actively encouraged. Every accepted speaker gets a speaker
  coach and two rehearsal slots."** That sentence is why I kept scrolling.
- **"As you write it. It is never split into first and last."** Under the name field on sign-up. The
  first sentence all evening written by somebody thinking about me.
- **My own name already in the speaker table as PRIMARY**, one action after making an account. The
  product knowing what it already knows.
- **The helper text under every field**, and especially *This is published* under the abstract — it
  answered the question I had before I had finished forming it.
- **The step pills.** *1. About you · 2. About your talk · 3. Logistics · 4. Review and submit*, with
  where I am marked. I always knew how far in I was.
- **The review screen** (`.walk/16-step4-review-and-submit.png`): every answer grouped by step, each
  group with its own Edit, skipped things honestly marked *Not answered* in grey. The best screen in
  the product, and the screen that saved me from submitting a consent I did not mean.
- **The timeline** — Submitted, Reviewed, A decision, Confirm your slot, Onboarding, On stage, each
  with a sentence saying what it will mean. It is the only thing in the product that gives waiting
  any texture at all.
- **"This proposal is already with the committee. The call is still open, so saving a change here
  updates it and resubmits it for you — a new revision the committee sees."**
  (`.walk/21-continue-editing-after-submit.png`). The best sentence in the product. It answers,
  unprompted, the exact question I had one screen earlier and could not get answered.
- **The conference's own front page** (`.walk/19-sitting3-event-home.png`) is genuinely handsome —
  wide, well-proportioned, with *Times: America/Los\_Angeles* spelled out in the practical box.

## Findings

### Fatal

**F1 · A first-time speaker's only draft cannot be reached from any screen.** *(bug — certain)*
`/portal`, Tuesday, 1440 × 900, `.walk/10-portal-return-desktop.png`. Signed in, one proposal, and
it is a draft. The portal names it, shows its status and its timeline — and the heading is not a
link, there is no *Continue*, `/portal/proposals` redirects here, the nav's *Your talks* points at
this page, and *Start a submission* on the CFP page redirects here too. Five links on the page, none
of them the one that matters. **Cost:** the journey ends. Everything after this point in my walk was
bought with a URL guess.
*What would have worked:* the same treatment the page already gives a **second** proposal — under
*Your other talks*, a draft's title **is** a link (`.walk/18-portal-after-submit.png`). Only the
lead talk loses it.

**F2 · The portal says Draft and Submitted about the same proposal, in adjacent lines.**
*(bug — certain)* `.walk/10-portal-return-desktop.png`. *Draft. Nothing is waiting on you.* above a
timeline whose **now** marker sits on **Submitted — To DevFlow Conf 2027 Call for Papers**.
**Cost:** I could not tell whether I had submitted, which is the only fact I came back for. A
speaker who believes the timeline closes the tab thinking they are done — with no title and no
abstract in the proposal. That is the worst outcome this product can produce, and it produces it
silently.

**F3 · Work typed on the public form is discarded without a word.** *(taste — certain)*
`.walk/02-cfp-preview-typed-into.png` → `.walk/03-login-after-start-submission.png`. Five answers,
including a sixty-word abstract, accepted by live fields styled exactly like the real ones, and gone
the moment I pressed the page's only button. Nothing acknowledged they had existed. **Cost:** five
answers re-typed; on a phone, that is the moment most people stop.
*What would have worked:* carry them into the draft, or make the fields read-only.

### Serious

**S3 · The conference disappears at sign-in and sign-up.** *(taste — certain)*

then tells them what they can do depends on what they have been *granted or invited to*.

**S4 · The sign-in screen's primary action is the one a first-timer cannot take.** *(taste —
certain)* `.walk/03`. Everybody arriving from a public call for papers is, by definition, likely to
be new. The black button is *Sign in*; *Create one* is small grey text beneath it.

**S5 · The public preview under-reports the form by four questions.** *(bug — certain)*

*(bug — high confidence)* `.walk/19`. Hero button and nav item both go to `/cfp/sponsor-sessions`

## ★☆☆☆☆

**I finished and lost work on the way, and the intended user could not have finished at all.** The
sentence that decided it is the one I read three times on Tuesday morning: *Draft. Nothing is
waiting on you.* — printed above a timeline claiming the talk was already submitted, on a page with
no way to open the draft it was describing. Everything was waiting on me. The talk had no title, no
abstract and no track, and the product's own summary of it was both wrong and unclickable.

This is the rating for this journey — a first-time speaker, three sittings, two devices, phone then
laptop — and not for the product. Almost every screen *inside* the wizard is three- or four-star
work, and two of them are five. That is exactly why the score is worth reporting rather than
softening: this is a good product with a missing link in the one place a speaker is most likely to
leave.

## The shortest path to the next star

Four changes. Nothing else is on this list.

1. **Link the lead talk.** The portal already renders a draft's title as a link — but only under
   *Your other talks*. Give the lead record the same `href` it already carries. Removes F1, which is
   the whole of the second sitting.
2. **Put the `now` marker on the stage the proposal has actually reached**, and stop a draft
   claiming *Nothing is waiting on you*. Removes F2, and D1 and D2 with it.
3. **Do not silently drop what somebody typed** — either carry the public form's answers into the
   draft, or make those fields read-only so nobody types into them. Removes F3.
4. **Refuse to make a second draft for a call the person already has a draft for**, and keep the
   `next` destination when an already-signed-in visitor presses *Start a submission*. Removes S1,
   which is what the guess in step 1's absence costs.

Those four take this journey from *cannot finish* to *finishes, having been told the truth* — one
star to three. The fourth star is a longer list, and its first four items are: name the conference
on sign-in and sign-up (S3); lead that screen with *Create an account* for anyone arriving from a
public call (S4); make the public preview honest about steps whose questions depend on later answers
(S5); and put the decision date on the timeline, so that waiting has a clock as well as a sentence.

## Read after the walk, changing nothing above

Broken character to locate the defects, for whoever picks this up. None of it revised a rating.

- **F1 is four characters of difference.** `workers/api/src/contexts/submissions/wizard.ts`:
  `otherTalks()` renders `<a href="${r.href}">${r.title}</a>`; `leadTalk()` does not, and
  `doThisFirst()` returns `raw("")` when the next action's urgency is `info` and there is no
  outstanding task — which is exactly the draft case. The `href` is already on the record.
- **Wall 3 is one line.** `contexts/identity/auth-routes.ts:42` — `if (ctx.person) return
  redirect(destinationFor(ctx));` discards the `?next=` an already-signed-in visitor arrived with.
  That is why *Start a submission* dumped me on `/portal` instead of the chooser.
- **S1's duplicate is unguarded.** `contexts/submissions/routes.ts` `POST /portal/proposals/new`
  calls `createDraft` with no check for an existing draft by the same person on the same CFP. The
  14 August walk reported the opposite behaviour; the difference is that walk went through the
  `/login?next=` path, which never reaches the POST.
- **D1's duplicated copy is literal.** `wizard.ts` `doThisFirst()` renders `action.label` as the
  `<h2>` and again as the `<p>`.
- **S8 is a deliberate rule with a wrong outcome.** `surfaces/public.ts` picks the *soonest-closing*
  call for the hero and nav link. In this fixture that is the sponsor-restricted call, so the one
  public call-to-action on the conference's front page sends speakers to a call they cannot use.
- The 14 August report found F1, F2, S3, S4, S5, S7, D3, D7, D8, D9, D10 independently. Everything
  it found is still there, unchanged, eight screens later. What this walk adds is F3, S1, S2, S6,
  S8, S9 and D1's duplication, and the observation that the portal *already knows how* to link a
  draft — it just does not do it for the only one a first-timer has.
