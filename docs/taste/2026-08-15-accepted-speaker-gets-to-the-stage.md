## What was not intuitive

- **I expected the product to lead with the live conference.** It leads with the archived one,
  with no way to switch and no indication that another exists. "Your talks" is a page about one
  talk.
- **I expected the step marked *now* to be the thing I could do.** It is the only step in the
  timeline that is inert.
- **I expected "100% complete / Nothing missing — thank you" to mean the conference has what it
  needs.** It means the boxes are non-empty. It read 100% before I uploaded a photo and 100%
  after, so the one deliverable I could identify and complete moved no number and changed no
  sentence anywhere in the product.
- **I expected "Saved." to mean the public page shows my new photo.** The profile page says, in
  its own words, "Your bio and headshot are what the public speaker page shows." It does not.
- **I expected the signed-in header to be consistent.** The home page shows a "Sign in" button
  in the body while showing "Sign out" in the header; the 2027 event page has a "Submit a talk"
  nav item that the 2027 speakers page does not; no public page links back to my talks.
- **I expected the hero button on the event page to be the real call.** "Submit a talk — closes
  10 Apr" goes to *Sponsor sessions*, while the list underneath says the Call for Papers closes
  30 April.

## What I lost by leaving and coming back

Measured against every point, in order.

1. **Is my work here?** Yes — nothing was lost, because nothing could be entered. The session
   itself survived three days on the phone, which is right.
2. **Do I know it is here without hunting?** No. Nothing on return said anything about me.
3. **Actions to get back to where I stopped?** **2** — and only because the signed-in home page
   has no link to my talks, so I had to go via Profile. Target is 1.
4. **Does it tell me what is left?** No. It never did.
5. **Does it still know what it knew?** The session, yes. The acceptance, never.
6. **Did anything change under me?** I cannot tell. There is no "since you were last here", no
   version, no date on anything in the portal except the timeline's own history.
7. **What arrived in between?** **Nothing.** No mail, no notification, no in-app anything. For a
   journey whose entire business purpose is chasing deliverables out of a speaker, the product
   sent me nothing in three days and I came back on guilt alone.

And the moment of leaving itself: **silence.** I closed the tab mid-hunt and the product neither
knew nor cared, which is defensible only because there was nothing to save.

## Moments of care

These are real and I would keep every one of them.

- **The privacy copy on the profile.** "Phone, dietary and accessibility notes are for logistics
  and never appear on any public page, whatever you set here." "Free text. Never inferred." next
  to Pronouns. "You can opt out of the directory and still speak. Only you can change this."
  Three sentences that answer the exact question a speaker has, before they ask it. This is the
  best writing in the product.
- **Nothing was retyped.** My headline, bio, company, links were all still there from last year,
  and the save kept every one of them. Zero fields the product already had were asked for again.
- **"Uploading again never overwrites — the previous version stays stored and listed."**
  Replacing an asset is explicitly not frightening, which is precisely the fear at this stage of
  the journey. (The "listed" half of the promise is not kept — see findings.)
- **"Saved."** — a green banner you cannot miss, at the top of the page, after one button press.
- **The CFP deadline:** "Open — closes 30 Apr 2027, 16:59 (America/Los_Angeles)." Date, time and
  timezone spelled out. The only deadline in the whole trip I could act on without converting
  anything.
- **"The form, inspectable before you sign up — try the dropdowns; nothing here is saved until
  you start a submission."** Kind, specific, and it removes a real fear.
- **"Star a session to build your own day — it stays in this browser"** and **"Published version
  1 · today. Changes are announced here and in the calendar feed."** Both are honest about where
  state lives and how you would hear about a change.
- **The portal timeline is a lovely idea, beautifully set** (`.walk/20-portal-desktop.png`).
  Submitted → Reviewed → Accepted → Confirm your slot → Onboarding → On stage, with the
  committee's actual words quoted back to me: *"Delighted to have this. The committee singled
  out how concrete it is."* Reading that was the single best moment of the trip. Everything
  wrong with this journey is wrong *around* this component, not in it.

## ★☆☆☆☆

**A speaker who was accepted cannot find, anywhere in this product, that she was accepted — and

## The shortest path to the next star

Ordered, in the order I hit them. The first two are load-bearing; without both, the journey
stays at one star.

1. **The portal must show every event I am in, live one first** — and when the only thing it has
   to show me is an archived talk from a finished conference, it must say so in those words
   rather than presenting it as current. F1.
2. **Make "Onboarding" a step I can open**, with the items behind it, their dates and their
   state. The product already says "anything else the event needs from you"; it has to be able
   to say *what*, and let me tap it. F3.
3. **Make the summary line derive from the timeline** so a screen cannot say "Nothing is waiting
   on you" while marking a step as *now*, and cannot mark a step *now* on a talk delivered three
   months ago. F2.

## Written after the ratings, having broken character

Nothing here changes anything above it. It is here because two of the findings have causes the
walk could not see, and whoever picks this up should not spend a day looking in the wrong place.

**The speaker onboarding surface exists, and it is good. This persona cannot reach it.**
`workers/api/src/contexts/submissions/wizard.ts` renders, for a speaker with open tasks: a "Do
this first" block with a single filled button and a sentence about what happens if the task is
not done, a "What we need from you" list with an *N of M done* count, per-task pages at
`/portal/tasks/{id}`, and an `otherTalks(...)` block for a speaker with more than one session.
`scripts/seed.mjs` defines eleven task types with due dates, reminder rules and blocking flags —
speaker agreement, code of conduct, profile and headshot, confirm title and abstract, recording
consent, AV requirements, travel details, **upload your final slides** ("PDF please, 16:9.
Re-uploading creates a new version — we keep the old ones"), tech check. Almost every question I
could not answer on the screen has an answer built for it somewhere in this repository.

**But `speaker@devflowconf.example` has no 2027 talk.** Priya appears once in the seed, as the
2026 keynote (`scripts/seed.mjs` line 2196, in `programme26`, with the comment that this is "the
talk her 2027 self is invited back on the strength of"). The 2027 proposals belong to dana,
marcus, ravi, elena, tom, aisha, kenji, lena, chris and nadia; the materialised task instances
are assigned to those people. So the persona the journeys file names as **the speaker** is the
one persona for whom the accepted-speaker surface never renders.

Three things follow, and I would weight them in this order:

1. **The demo world cannot demonstrate its own best feature to the person it is for.** Anybody
   who signs in as the seeded speaker to see what Podium does for speakers sees the screen I
   saw. That is a fixture defect with the blast radius of a product defect.
2. **F1 and F2 are still real and still on the screen.** Even granting that Priya has only an
   archived talk, `/portal` presents a session from an archived 2026 event with an "Onboarding"
   step marked **now**, an "On stage" date three months in the past, and a summary line saying
   nothing is waiting — with no word anywhere that this conference is over. The state machine is
   being rendered as if the event were live. Nothing about the seed excuses that.
3. **F4 has an explanation and still needs a sentence on the screen.**
   `contexts/scheduling/publication.ts` builds the public speaker page from a published snapshot
   and only carries a headshot whose `scan_status` is `clean` (the comment cites INV-11-3). So
   the delay is deliberate and probably right. The speaker is told none of it: the profile page
   says flatly "Your bio and headshot are what the public speaker page shows", and after
   "Saved." my public page still had the old face with no explanation, no "it will appear when
   the programme is next published", and no way to tell the difference between *working as
   designed* and *lost*.
