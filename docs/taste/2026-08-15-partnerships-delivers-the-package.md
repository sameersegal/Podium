# Partnerships delivers the package

**Slug:** `partnerships-delivers-the-package` · **Walked:** 15 August 2026 · **Viewport:** 1440 × 900 throughout
**Rating: ★☆☆☆☆**

Two roles, two sittings, against the shipped seed. Nothing was edited; the one thing I had to
arrange — a way in for the sponsor's contact — I arranged through the product's own screens, as
Morgan, and the report says what that turned out to be.

---

## Who I was, and what I came to do

**Sitting 1 — Morgan Diaz, partnerships.** I sold this year's sponsor packages myself: Ferro
Labs, Keelworks, Brightpath. I have a call with each of them this week and on every call
somebody asks me a version of the same question. I came to be able to answer it without saying
"let me check and come back to you": **what did you buy, what have you used, and what is still
owed — by me to you, and by you to me?** Then I had to get Omar Reyes at Ferro Labs into this
thing so he can do his own bit. What done looks like: three short paragraphs I could read aloud,
and an email in Omar's inbox. **What I'd do instead:** the signed PDFs in Drive and the
spreadsheet I keep. That is what I did last year, and if this is slower than the spreadsheet I
quietly stop keeping this one current.

**Sitting 2 — Omar Reyes, marketing manager at Ferro Labs.** I did not choose this tool. Somebody
signed a sponsorship and it landed on me. An email says I need to give a speaker's name — Dele
Okonjo, Principal Engineer — by a date. I have twenty minutes between meetings. **What I'd do
instead:** reply to that email and ask the conference person to type it in for me. That is one
click away the entire time.

**Did I finish?** Morgan, half: I can state each sponsor's position, but only by assembling it
from four screens, and I have to hedge on the one date that matters. **Omar: no.** There is no
screen in this product where he can name a speaker. He gave up and replied to the email.

The severe version of this is on Morgan's side: **if I had pressed Back before copying that URL,
my paying customer's only route in would have been destroyed**, with no way to tell whether a

**Fatal · Bug · Certain**

## ★☆☆☆☆

**The sentence that decided it:** the paying customer was given an account, walked to a page
headed *DO THIS FIRST*, and found nothing on it he could press — so the one job the whole
sponsorship context exists to deliver cannot be done by the person who paid for it.

Morgan's half is a solid three on its own — the sponsorships list is the best-written screen in

two: Omar cannot finish, and the screen that should have told him so told him the opposite.

## The shortest path to the next star

Ordered, minimal, in the order I hit them. The first three are one star's worth on their own.

1. **Put an "Add a speaker" form on `/portal/proposals/:id` for a sponsor contact whose session
   has none.** The capability exists; the affordance does not. Without this the journey cannot
   leave one star.
2. **Stop telling a sponsor contact "Nothing is waiting on you" when their session has no
   speaker named** — and name the deadline on that line, in the event's timezone.
3. **Say "Ferro Labs" to Omar.** The invitation page and the portal should both name the company
   and the package, so he can tell in three seconds that he is in the right place.
4. **Add "Resend invitation" to the contact row**, with a copy button on the link and a line
   saying when it was issued and whether it has been used — and change "contact invited" to
   something that does not imply mail was sent.
5. **Render entitlement deadlines as dates in the event's timezone** on the one screen Morgan
   reads them from.

## Written after the ratings, having broken character

Nothing below changed a rating.

- **There is no sponsor-facing surface in this product.** Every route in
  `workers/api/src/contexts/sponsorship/routes.ts` is under `/admin/*` or `/v1/*`. A sponsor
  contact's entire signed-in world is the speaker portal. F3 is structural, not a missing link.
- **The capability behind F1 exists and is not rendered.**
  `POST /portal/proposals/:id/speakers` adds a co-speaker and even returns a one-time accept
  URL. The read view is called as `proposalReadView(detail, next, affordance.editable)`, and
  `editAffordance` in `packages/domain/src/submissions/types.ts:118` returns
  `{ editable: false, reason: "An accepted proposal cannot be edited." }` for an accepted
  proposal. So the form is correctly suppressed by a rule — **and the `reason` string it
  produces is never shown to the user either.** Omar got neither the form nor the explanation,
  which is why the page reads as broken rather than as closed.
- **The "Not answered" contradiction (F7)** is the seeded sponsor proposal carrying a title and
  abstract on the proposal row while having no wizard *answers*; the panel renders the form's
  fields, so all four read "Not answered" next to data that plainly exists.
- **The invite panel is deliberate.** `routes.ts` carries the comment *"INV-01-15: the one-time
  accept link is shown on screen, never redirected past."* Showing it once is a decision, not an
  oversight — but the decision is about the token's lifetime, and what it costs the user is that
  a paying customer's only route in lives in one person's clipboard, with no way to reissue from
  the row and no record that it happened.
